Strictly speaking, there is no radiation-free food. Plants pull natural radionuclides from the soil, animals eat the plants — and both end up on our plates. On average, everyone takes in an effective dose of about **0.3 millisieverts per year** through food. That's completely normal, and it has always been this way.

## The banana as a yardstick

The most famous example is the banana: rich in potassium, and a tiny, fixed fraction of all potassium on Earth is radioactive **potassium-40**. On paper, one banana works out to about **0.1 microsieverts** — physicists jokingly call it the "banana equivalent dose," a way to make tiny radiation amounts tangible.

Here's the twist: your body keeps its potassium balance constant. Eat more potassium, excrete more potassium. Bananas don't "irradiate" anyone — they just illustrate how ubiquitous natural radioactivity is. Potatoes, nuts, dried fruit all carry potassium-40, and the human body itself hums along at several thousand becquerels.

## The outliers

A few foods stand out:

- **[Brazil nuts](/en/articles/brazil-nuts)** concentrate radium through the tree's roots — the champion among plant foods.
- **Wild mushrooms and wild boar** from certain regions of Europe still contain cesium-137 from the 1986 Chernobyl accident. Commercial products are monitored; only people who very frequently eat self-gathered mushrooms or game from affected areas raise their dose noticeably.
- **[Mineral water](/en/articles/mineral-water)** can carry more or less radium and uranium depending on the source — mainly relevant for infant formula.

## Your plate in perspective

| Source | Dose per year (approx.) |
| --- | --- |
| All food combined (average) | 0.3 mSv |
| 2 Brazil nuts daily | +0.16 mSv |
| Weekly wild-mushroom meals from an affected area | up to a few tenths of a mSv |
| For comparison: radon at home (typical) | often 1–3+ mSv |

The message is reassuring: what we eat plays a bit part in our radiation exposure. If you actually want to cut your dose, don't start with the fruit bowl — start with [radon in your house](https://unseenhouse.com/en/articles/what-is-radon), or with smoking, the [most underrated radiation source of all](/en/articles/smoking-radiation).
