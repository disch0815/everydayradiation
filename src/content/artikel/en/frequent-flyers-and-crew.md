For occasional travelers, cosmic radiation is a curiosity. For people who work in airplanes, it's a measurable occupational reality: **aircrew are among the most radiation-exposed workers of any profession.** According to CDC/NIOSH, the annual added dose for pilots and cabin crew typically runs **0.2 to 5 millisieverts** depending on routes — more than most workers in hospital X-ray departments receive.

## How the dose adds up

Flying 600–900 block hours a year means spending weeks on end at 33,000–39,000 feet, where the dose rate is about 100 times the sea-level value. On polar long-haul routes it accumulates fastest: a single [flight to New York](/en/articles/flight-dose-new-york) adds about 0.05 mSv — at ten rotations a month, that quickly becomes a meaningful annual dose.

## How crews are protected

In Europe, airlines are required to **monitor and document** their crews' radiation exposure — usually computed from route, altitude, and solar activity — under the same rules as other radiation workers, with a 20 mSv annual limit. In the U.S., the FAA officially considers aircrew occupationally exposed and publishes guidance and dose-calculation tools, though no binding federal dose limit applies; NIOSH actively studies crew health.

One special case matters most: **pregnancy**. NIOSH research links first-trimester cosmic radiation doses above 0.36 mSv to increased miscarriage risk, and European rules impose strict dose limits for the unborn child once a pregnancy is declared. Many airlines move pregnant crew to ground duty entirely.

## And frequent flyers without the uniform?

A passenger who commutes intercontinentally every week can reach crew-like doses — with nobody monitoring them. It's still no cause for panic: even an extra 5 mSv per year is comparable to the natural annual dose many people receive anyway. If you want your personal number, run your routes through the [flight dose calculator](https://kurse.dirkschulze.ch/flugdosis/) — leg by leg.

The closing perspective: for most people, the lung cancer risk from [radon in their own home](https://unseenhouse.com/en/articles/what-is-radon) outweighs any amount of air travel.
